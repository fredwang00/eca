## FOG Patterns

**Structural pattern:** Prepared remarks from co-CEOs (primarily Sarandos, secondarily Peters) carry consistently higher FOG than CFO sections or Q&A. This gap has persisted across all nine quarters without material improvement.

**Recurring phrases, identified by quarter of first appearance:**

- *"We're feeling really good about the business"* / *"We feel good about our progress"* / *"We are as energized as ever"* — Q3 2025, but variants appear in Q2 2023 through Q4 2025. Pure sentiment signal; zero falsifiable content.
- *"Multi-year build / multi-year progress"* — Q3 2023. A self-refreshing time horizon that perpetually defers accountability.
- *"Constantly iterating / continual improvement / getting better and better"* — Q2 2023 through Q4 2025. No mechanism, no benchmark, no rate of improvement specified.
- *"Thrilling our members"* / *"bringing joy to members"* — Q4 2023 through Q3 2025. Used as a strategic summary when strategic reasoning was requested.
- *"Flywheel"* — Q1 2024 through Q4 2025. Deployed repeatedly without defining the rate of spin, the inputs, or the measurable outputs.
- *"We're only X% of the addressable market"* — Every quarter from Q2 2023 onward. Legitimate framing device, but reflexively deployed as an optimism anchor regardless of current business conditions. Functions as a permanently available excuse rather than a planning assumption.
- *"Crawl, walk, run"* (advertising) — Q3 2025. A new sequencing cliché that, like "multi-year build," embeds its own indefinite deferral into the language.
- *"Just doing the work"* (advertising roadmap) — Q4 2024. Confidence substituting for specificity.

**Linguistic precision trajectory:** Degraded from Q2 2023 to Q4 2023, partially recovered through Q3 2024, then regressed in Q2–Q3 2025 as prepared remarks became more promotional alongside strong financial results. The pattern suggests FOG correlates inversely with operational pressure: when results are strong, discipline in language relaxes.

**One genuine improvement:** Advertising FOG has decreased as the business has matured. From "building capabilities" and "knocking these down one after another" (Q2 2023) to specific CPM characterizations, fill rate data, programmatic partner names, and an absolute revenue target of $3 billion for 2026 (Q4 2025). This is measurable linguistic progress in one domain.

## Flags & Risks

**Data quality flags:**
- Free cash flow not mentioned in Q4 2024 earnings call — a notable omission for a company that has historically foregrounded this metric.
- Advertising revenue base figure withheld in absolute terms through Q3 2025; first absolute anchor ($3 billion 2026 target) appears in Q4 2025.
- "Primary quality metric" cited as all-time high in Q4 2025 but never defined or disclosed to investors.
- Subscriber count reporting retired in Q1 2024 without a disclosed baseline; directional Q2 subscriber question refused in same call as retirement announcement.
- Warner Bros. integration cost, synergy timeline, and post-close financial model not disclosed in Q4 2025.

**Communication-level risks, aggregated:**

1. **Structural absence of voluntary problem disclosure.** Zero instances across nine quarters of an executive proactively surfacing a named initiative that failed, missed a target, or was abandoned with an honest explanation. The only negative disclosure in the series was the Brazil CIDE tax (Q3 2025), which was involuntary (court ruling). This is the single most consistent pattern in the dataset and the primary ceiling on candor scores.

2. **Perishable goal substitution.** The 100M borrowing household estimate (Q2 2023) was never reconciled with conversion actuals. Gaming milestones are structurally unaccountable — "proof points" are cited without defining the proof required. "Critical scale" in advertising countries (Q3 2024) has no quantitative definition. Each quarter introduces new forward framing that cannot be verified because the prior framing was never closed out.

3. **Metric retirement risk.** Quarterly subscriber count retired Q1 2024. Engagement metrics have shifted denominators ("owner household basis" introduced Q2 2024 defensively). "Primary quality metric" exists but is not public. The pattern of retiring, reframing, or qualifying metrics under pressure is an escalating disclosure risk.

4. **Two-CEO accountability diffusion.** Hard questions migrate between Sarandos and Peters without a consistent ownership pattern. Financial commitments belong to Neumann. No single executive owns a specific operational outcome. This structure provides natural diffusion when results disappoint.

5. **Sports strategy language softening.** From "not a signal of any change to our sports strategy" (Q4 2023) to "if there was a path where we could make the economics work... we certainly would explore" (Q4 2024). The position has softened with each quarter that live events perform well. Monitor for whether the boundary disappears entirely if a major rights window opens.

6. **Warner Bros. regulatory overconfidence.** Sarandos offered unqualified confidence in approval ("we're confident we're gonna be able to secure all the approvals") without acknowledging any scenario involving conditions, behavioral remedies, or delays. For a $9.7 billion transaction reviewed by DOJ and EC, that framing warrants scrutiny.

## Verify Next Quarter

From the Q4 2025 tracking commitments, verify the following in the Q1 2026 call:

1. **Ad revenue trajectory toward $3 billion in 2026.** Peters committed explicitly to "roughly doubling" ads revenue to approximately $3 billion in 2026. Q1 run-rate disclosure (even directional) should be checked against this anchor. Watch for whether absolute figures are disclosed or whether the language reverts to percentage growth without a base.

2. **Operating margin 31.5% full-year 2026.** With M&A costs embedded as a 0.5-point drag, verify whether Q1 actuals and full-year guidance reaffirmation hold this target, and whether any Warner Bros. integration costs are reclassified in ways that complicate period comparisons.

3. **Warner Bros. regulatory status.** Sarandos expressed unqualified confidence in approval. Any conditional approval, behavioral remedy, or timeline extension should be evaluated against this statement. Absence of any update is itself informative.

4. **"Primary quality metric" disclosure.** This metric has been cited repeatedly as "all-time high" without public definition. Press management to name the metric, its measurement methodology, and the historical series. If Q1 2026 cites it again without definition, flag as an escalating accountability gap.

5. **Cloud gaming expansion to TV.** Peters stated roughly one-third of members have TV-based game access, with expansion planned for 2026. Verify whether uptake data from the party pack launch is revisited and whether the "10% reach into eligible members" baseline is updated.

6. **Warner Bros. post-close financial model.** If the acquisition closes before Q1 2026 reporting, verify whether management discloses integration cost estimates, assumed synergies, and timeline to accretion — none of which appeared in the Q4 2025 call despite the deal's scale.
