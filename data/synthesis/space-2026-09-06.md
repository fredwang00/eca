### Coverage: Q1 2023 – Q2 2026 | Companies: RKLB, ASTS

## 1. Candor Trajectory

**Sector summary:** One company stagnating at B, one locked at C+. The gap between them has not narrowed across 28 combined quarters of observation.

**RKLB:** Oscillates between 2.65 and 3.125, with a single outlier quarter (Q3 2023: 3.625) driven by the Electron mission anomaly disclosure — ten minutes of Paschen curve physics, named failure conditions, and a committed return-to-flight window. No other quarter in the dataset approaches that level of voluntary negative disclosure. The grade fell back to B the following quarter and has not recovered. The structural cause is stable and identified: Spice's financial sections consistently score B+; Beck's prepared remarks consistently score C. Twelve quarters of observation confirm this gap has not narrowed. The CFO is doing the disclosure work the CEO is not.

**ASTS:** Locked in a 37-basis-point band (2.15–2.75) for fourteen consecutive quarters. The only inflection — Q4 2023 at 2.75 — reflected briefly more accountable CFO-level disclosure that did not persist. The trough (Q3 2024: 2.15) coincided with zero commercial revenue and maximum opacity on financing. Q1–Q2 2026 show a deteriorating Capital Stewardship grade at precisely the moment of maximum cash burn — the inverse of what accountability should look like as a company approaches commercial launch.

**Cross-company pattern:** Both companies exhibit the same structural split: CFO disclosure is consistently stronger than CEO/President narrative. At RKLB, the gap is graded and named. At ASTS, it is more extreme — Klingler/Milstein's prepared remarks systematically undermine whatever specificity Wallace provides. Neither company has closed this internal gap in three years.

**Outlier in the sector:** RKLB's Q3 2023 (3.625) stands as the only A-grade quarter across both companies in 28 quarters of coverage. It is an anomaly produced by a failure, not a success — which is itself the signal. Neither company has reached A-territory through routine disclosure.

## 3. Bottleneck Migration

The two companies are at different points on the operational maturity curve, producing different bottleneck language — but the migration within each company is trackable.

**RKLB bottleneck trajectory:**
- Q1–Q4 2023: **Manufacturing throughput** — Electron cadence, Photon capacity, production rate limits were the dominant constraint language.
- Q1–Q4 2024: **Neutron development timeline** — Engine testing (Archimedes), stage fabrication, pad construction at LC-3. Manufacturing constraint migrated from Electron to the new vehicle.
- Q1–Q2 2025: **Component supply and hiring** — SDA Tranche 2 execution referenced supply chain on specific satellite subsystems; headcount growth cited as a pace constraint on Space Systems.
- Q3 2025 – Q2 2026: **Neutron schedule and capital** — The dominant constraint language shifted from technical readiness to financing capacity and timeline management. Neutron has consumed ~$360M vs. a $250–300M IPO estimate; the primary disclosed bottleneck is now **capital allocation between Neutron, M&A (Iridium, Mynaric, Motiv, Geost), and Electron fleet maintenance** rather than any single technical barrier.

**ASTS bottleneck trajectory:**
- Q1 2023 – Q2 2024: **Capital access** — The dominant constraint language was financing. Calls centered on loan facilities, equity raises, and the Block 1 satellite cost ($100M → $115M). Launch was secondary because capital to build the satellites was the binding constraint.
- Q3 2024 – Q2 2025: **Manufacturing scale** — Capital became less binding as AT&T/Verizon prepayments and equity raises arrived. The bottleneck language shifted to per-satellite cost ($16–18M → $21–23M across three revisions) and production rate. Six satellites per month target, introduced as H2 2025, became the primary tracking metric.
- Q3 2025 – Q2 2026: **Launch cadence and orbital population** — With 13 BlueBirds confirmed and 45 targeted by early 2027, the constraint language has migrated to **launch slot availability and satellite delivery rates**. CapEx is now the disclosed binding variable ($622M in Q2 2026 alone), with the bottleneck being whether manufacturing and launches can achieve the orbit-population rate required to support the 2026–2027 revenue ramp.

**Cross-company observation:** Both companies have migrated away from single-point technical bottlenecks toward systemic capital deployment constraints. This is a maturation signal, but it also means neither company has a single falsifiable constraint to resolve — the bottleneck is now diffuse enough to explain any miss across multiple dimensions simultaneously.

## 4. Cross-Company Corroboration

### Corroborated Signals

**Government demand for commercial space capacity (SDA specifically):**
Both RKLB (SDA Tranche 2: $489M; Tranche 3: undisclosed) and ASTS ($43M SDA contract, $30M SDA award) independently confirm SDA as an active, paying customer. This is one of the sector's most concrete demand signals — two companies, named agency, disclosed dollars, disclosed milestone structures. Government demand for commercial satellite capability is corroborated.

**Revenue-share as the dominant commercial telecom model:**
Both companies independently describe their MNO agreements as revenue-share structures rather than fixed-price service contracts. RKLB's Kepler booking at $50–55M per Neutron mission is a different model (launch, not service), but ASTS's AT&T and Verizon structures both involve revenue participation. Neither company has disclosed the economics of their share. The convergence on revenue-share as a commercial structure — rather than subscriptions, fixed fees, or spectrum leases — is notable and shared.

**Capital access as competitive moat language:**
Both management teams have introduced "financial strength" as a strategic differentiator in recent quarters (RKLB's $2.1B verified cash, Q1 2026; ASTS's $3.0B verified cash, Q1 2026). Neither company frames the cash as operational runway against a burn rate — both frame it as competitive positioning. The narrative convergence is suspicious given that both companies are burning cash at accelerating rates.

### Unverified / Single-Source Claims

**ASTS: "Over $100M in funded near-term government value across 2026–2027"** (Q2 2026):
No counterparty named, no agency named, no award structure disclosed. RKLB makes no corroborating reference to this government demand pool. ASTS is the sole source; it has not been validated by a named counterparty in any public disclosure.

**ASTS: Rakuten J-LEO ~$1B "preliminary selection"** (Q2 2026):
Named counterparty, but status is "preliminary selection, pending government approvals and final agreements." Not in the $1.2B contracted backlog. Rakuten has not made a corresponding public announcement confirming the selection as of the covered period. Flag as unverified until a definitive agreement is signed.

**RKLB: Kepler first Neutron commercial booking:**
Kepler is a named counterparty. However, Kepler has not publicly confirmed the booking in any source cross-referenced in these transcripts. The $50–55M ASP range is management-stated. Flag as partially verified (named counterparty, single-source disclosure).

**RKLB: "Half a dozen M&A deals in pipeline"** (Spice, Q1 2025):
No subsequent quarter produced six disclosed acquisitions. By Q2 2026, the confirmed M&A pipeline is Iridium (announced), Mynaric (pending court approval), Motiv (pending), and Geost (pending). "Half a dozen" appears to have been a loose figure. The count is single-source and has not been corroborated.

**ASTS: $1.2B contracted revenue backlog** (Q3 2025 – Q2 2026):
Presented with headline confidence across four consecutive quarters. Management explicitly refused counterparty-level disaggregation. The identifiable named components — AT&T ($20M prepayment, economics undisclosed), Verizon ($100M), stc ($175M), SDA ($43M + $30M) — sum to approximately $368M in named, disclosed commitments against a claimed $1.2B. The remaining ~$830M is unverifiable from public disclosures. This is not a data error; it is a deliberate disclosure boundary. The claim is unverifiable.

## 6. Aggregated Flags & Risks

### Risks Appearing Across Both Companies

**1. ATM/equity issuance reflexivity:**
Both companies have used at-the-money equity programs as primary liquidity mechanisms. RKLB raised $1.08B via ATM in Q2 2026 alone; total equity grew from $382.5M (Q4 2024) to $3,492.1M (Q2 2026) — almost entirely financing-driven. ASTS's equity issuances are the primary driver of its cash position growth. Neither company has named the circularity: elevated stock prices enable the equity raises that fund operations that sustain the narrative that supports the stock price. Neither management team has disclosed what happens to the capital plan if the equity premium compresses. **This is the sector's most systemic undisclosed risk.**

**2. Perishable goal pattern:**
Both companies introduce strategic themes without retiring prior ones. RKLB: "Constellation owner and operator" (8+ quarters, zero milestones); Flatellite (announced Q4 2024, unspecified through Q2 2026); space data centers and three other themes (Q4 2025, untested as of Q2 2026). ASTS: Export credit financing (described as "progressing nicely" from Q3 2024 through Q2 2026 without a single disclosed counterparty); AI compute/IoT/radar adjacencies (introduced Q4 2025, no timelines). The compounding effect is that each quarter's call adds new themes to a growing queue of unverified prior commitments, making accountability structurally harder.

**3. Cash figure presentation discrepancy:**
ASTS management cited ~$3.5B in Q1 2026 against verified $3,029.6M — a $470M gap. In Q2 2026, the pro forma figure of $3.7B exceeded verified cash of $2,288.3M by ~$1.4B. RKLB does not have a comparable discrepancy in the structured data, but the ATM termination rationale in Q2 2026 is unexplained. Both companies have introduced liquidity framing that systematically overstates the verified balance sheet position. **Investors relying on management-cited cash figures for runway analysis are working from materially incorrect inputs.**

**4. Cost escalation without internal attribution:**
ASTS per-satellite cost revised from $16–18M → $19–21M → $21–23M across three cycles, each attributed to external factors (customer orbit change, launch costs, tariffs). RKLB Neutron cumulative spend is ~$360M vs. $250–300M IPO estimate — attributed to timeline extension at $15M/quarter in human capital costs. Neither company has examined the forecasting methodology that produced the original estimate. Both companies are pattern-matching: revision acknowledged, external cause named, internal estimation error not examined.

**5. Revenue ramp arithmetic that doesn't close:**
ASTS entered 2026 with $14.7M in Q1 revenue against $150–200M full-year guidance — requiring a 3–4x ramp in three quarters with no quarterly cadence provided. RKLB's operating losses have remained stable at -$51M to -$60M across all disclosed quarters despite revenue nearly doubling from Q4 2024 ($132M) to Q2 2026 ($234M) — suggesting that growth is not converting to leverage. Neither company has provided a profitability bridge that closes within a disclosed timeframe.

### Risks Isolated to One Company

**RKLB only:**
- Neutron timeline credibility (five generations of slip, now Q4 2026 pad delivery); no systematic forecasting accountability offered
- Segment guidance elimination (Q3 2024 onward) reduces investors' ability to verify margin composition
- Iridium integration plan described as "early-stage" with no disclosed synergy, operational model, or capital requirement
- GAAP operating loss of -$57.5M omitted from Q2 2026 prepared remarks and Q&A — material oral disclosure gap

**ASTS only:**
- Seven consecutive quarters of sub-$2M revenue through Q2 2025 against accelerating CapEx; commercial service revenue has not yet materialized at scale
- $1.2B contracted backlog explicitly refuses counterparty-level disaggregation — unverifiable as a receivable
- Q2 2026 FCF of -$719.2M provides approximately 3 quarters of runway at current verified cash levels without additional raises
- Convertible notes at $149.20 effective conversion presented as a "triumph" without acknowledging debt maturity risk if conversion is not achieved
- Revenue-share model shift when ASTS deploys its own spectrum (Ligado L-band, S-Band) deferred for five consecutive quarters — active evasion pattern

### Gerstner Check

**The sector is generating $1.29B in revenue against $2.88B in free cash flow burn over seven quarters.** CapEx-to-revenue ratio: RKLB 0.20x (declining trend, from 0.16x in Q4 2024 to 0.11x in Q2 2026 — showing leverage); ASTS 17.7x (Q2 2026 CapEx alone exceeded seven quarters of cumulative revenue). These are not comparable businesses at comparable stages.

**RKLB's capital deployment pattern is consistent with a company approaching operating leverage:** Revenue doubled over the seven-quarter window while CapEx remained relatively contained (range: $21–50M/quarter). Operating losses are stable at -$51M to -$60M — poor, but stable. The concern is not capital efficiency; it is that growth is not converting to margin improvement despite the scale increase.

**ASTS's capital deployment pattern is consistent with a pre-revenue infrastructure build:** $2.1B in CapEx deployed against $119M in cumulative revenue is a bet on future cash flows, not a current business. Q2 2026's FCF of -$719.2M in a single quarter against verified cash of $2,288.3M implies approximately three quarters of runway without additional capital activity. The company has demonstrated it can raise capital; it has not demonstrated it can earn it.

**The pundit claim that the commercial space sector is "generating momentum toward profitability" is challenged by this data.** At the sector level, seven quarters of combined operations have produced $1.29B in revenue and $2.88B in cash consumption. The ratio of cash burned to revenue earned is 2.24:1 across the sector, and it is worsening — driven entirely by ASTS's accelerating CapEx. RKLB's trajectory, viewed in isolation, is more credible as a path toward eventual profitability. ASTS's trajectory is a capital markets story, not yet an operating story.
