### Period: Q1 2023 – Q2 2026

## 1. Candor Trajectory

**Sector pattern: One stagnator, one chronic underperformer — no improving company in the cohort.**

RKLB has held a persistent B band (2.65–3.125) across 12 quarters, peaking once at 3.625 (Q3 2023) and never approaching that level again. ASTS has held a C+ band (2.15–2.75) across 14 quarters, with a single upward excursion to 2.75 in Q4 2023 that reversed immediately. Neither company shows a positive trend.

**Spread is meaningful and widening.** RKLB's 12-quarter average score is approximately 2.90. ASTS's 14-quarter average is approximately 2.46. The gap — roughly 0.44 points — represents a real qualitative difference in disclosure behavior, not rounding noise. As ASTS enters commercial service and capital stakes rise, its scores have not improved: Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 both printed 2.28 and 2.40, bracketing the all-time low of 2.15 set in Q3 2024.

**What drove the peaks:**
- RKLB Q3 2023 (3.625): Peter Beck's unprompted 10-minute Paschen-arc failure disclosure — named the four physical conditions causing failure, described the test regime, accepted engineering accountability. This is the sector's highest-candor moment in the full dataset.
- ASTS Q4 2023 (2.75): CFO financial segment improved substantially; more specific operational disclosures accompanied the Block 1 launch preparation. Driven by one executive's behavior, not a company-wide shift.

**What drives the floors:**
- RKLB's 2.65 quarters (Q1 2023, Q1 2024, Q3 2024, Q2 2025, Q4 2025) share a common structure: FOG accumulation in prepared remarks accumulates to C-level density while Q&A holds at B. The composite averages down.
- ASTS's 2.15 (Q3 2024) and 2.28 (Q3 2023, Q1 2026) represent the sector's weakest disclosure quarters. Q3 2024 coincided with manufacturing delays and capital uncertainty — precisely when candor should peak and instead bottomed.

**Structural divergence between executives:** In both companies, the CFO/financial segment consistently outperforms CEO/President prepared remarks on precision. This is unusual and signals deliberate promotional register in executive scripting rather than company-wide communication philosophy.

## 3. Bottleneck Migration

Neither company generates sufficient quarterly disclosure to map classic industrial bottleneck language (chips, power, permitting) in the way a hyperscaler cohort would. However, both companies exhibit a distinct pattern: **the stated constraint migrates toward whatever recently caused the most visible slip.**

**RKLB bottleneck evolution:**

| Period | Stated Constraint |
|---|---|
| Q1–Q2 2023 | Component supply, launch cadence |
| Q3 2023 | Engine reliability (Paschen arc disclosed) |
| Q4 2023–Q1 2024 | SDA manufacturing ramp, supplier delivery timing |
| Q2–Q3 2024 | Neutron third-party supplier failures; procurement lead times |
| Q4 2024–Q1 2025 | Tank rupture investigation; AFP qualification |
| Q2 2025 | M&A integration (Mynaric, Geost) bandwidth |
| Q3–Q4 2025 | Neutron Stage 1 qualification; launch site construction pace |

The pattern: each constraint is described as singular and resolvable. No call has introduced a systematic analysis of how RKLB's supply chain models for serial risk. The Neutron budget overrun (30%+ disclosed proactively in Q3 2025) is the exception — a voluntary structural acknowledgment. Everything else is presented as isolated.

**ASTS bottleneck evolution:**

| Period | Stated Constraint |
|---|---|
| Q1–Q3 2023 | Satellite design validation; SpaceX launch slot |
| Q4 2023–Q1 2024 | Block 1 launch execution; regulatory approval |
| Q2–Q3 2024 | Manufacturing ramp; per-satellite cost control |
| Q4 2024–Q1 2025 | Launch frequency; BlueBird production throughput |
| Q2 2025–Q1 2026 | Manufacturing cadence (6/month target deferring) |
| Q2 2026 | Launch vehicle availability; regulatory spectrum (Ligado) |

**Sector-level migration signal:** Both companies have shifted from "validation/design" constraints (2023) to "manufacturing ramp/throughput" constraints (2024–2025) to "cadence and integration" constraints (2026). This is a coherent progression consistent with companies moving from development to early production. The risk is that each company presents the migration as proof of progress rather than evidence that the prior constraint was underestimated. ASTS's six-satellites-per-month target has deferred at least once without explicit acknowledgment; RKLB's Neutron timeline has slipped four times with partial acknowledgment.

## 4. Cross-Company Corroboration

### Corroborated Signals

**1. SDA as anchor customer:** Both RKLB (SDA T2 $489M prime) and ASTS (SDA $43M contract, Q4 2024) independently cite the Space Development Agency as a named, signed contract partner. This independently confirms SDA is actively contracting with commercial space companies across launch and space systems — not a single-source relationship.

**2. Spectrum as a live constraint:** ASTS's Ligado/Legato acquisition and ongoing regulatory navigation corroborate the view that spectrum access — not just satellite hardware — is a binding constraint for direct-to-cell architectures. RKLB does not face this constraint directly but its Neutron manifest (HASTE/MACH-TB government missions) similarly depends on government contractual vehicles that can be delayed by administrative processes. Both companies are exposed to non-technical delays outside engineering.

**3. Equity dilution as the primary funding mechanism:** Both companies are funding operations primarily through equity issuance and prepayments, not operating cash flow. RKLB's ATM programs ($303.8M Q2 2025, $468.8M Q3 2025); ASTS's convertible notes ($1.15B Q2 2026) and ongoing equity offerings. Neither company has demonstrated a path to self-funding operations. This is a sector-level structural signal, not an isolated company choice.

### Unverified / Single-Source Claims

**ASTS: "~60 MNO partners covering 3 billion subscribers"**
No counterparty in the dataset independently confirms a commercial agreement with ASTS beyond AT&T (confirmed by AT&T's own announcements), Verizon (confirmed), and Vodafone (confirmed). The remaining 57 claimed partners have not been independently corroborated in any public filing or counterparty disclosure identified across 14 quarters of coverage. Flag: **unverified at scale.**

**ASTS: "$1.2B in contracted revenue commitments"**
Cited Q1 and Q2 2026 without disaggregation. Against named signed deals, the verifiable total is ~$295M in commercial prepayments plus $43M SDA. The gap of ~$862M is unexplained and unfiled. Flag: **material discrepancy requiring reconciliation.**

**RKLB: Neutron demand pipeline ("drinking from a fire hydrant")**
No counterparty beyond the first unnamed commercial customer (ASP ~$50–55M) has been identified across eight quarters of analyst questioning. SpaceX and ULA have not confirmed competitive displacement. Flag: **unverified demand depth.**

**RKLB: Golden Dome adjacency**
The $175B program is a government initiative. RKLB's positioning as prime, sub, or component supplier simultaneously has not been corroborated by any government announcement, RFP selection, or program office disclosure. Flag: **aspirational framing without government confirmation.**

## 6. Aggregated Flags & Risks

### Risks Appearing Across Both Companies

**1. Signed-vs.-aspirational conflation**
Both companies blend confirmed contracts with aspirational pipeline in prepared remarks without clear demarcation. RKLB cites MACH-TB 2.0's $1.45B ceiling as a credibility signal without disclosing its specific share. ASTS cites "$1.2B in contracted revenue commitments" without counterparty breakdown. Investors cannot distinguish contracted from conditional from aspirational without external reconstruction. **Severity: High. Both companies.**

**2. Operating cash generation deferred indefinitely**
RKLB has negative FCF in every structured-data quarter (Q4 2024–Q4 2025), ranging -$23.9M to -$114.2M. ASTS has negative FCF in every structured-data quarter (Q4 2024–Q2 2026), ranging -$110.5M to -$719.2M. Neither company has disclosed a specific quarter in which FCF turns positive with assumptions attached. ASTS's "operating cash flow positive at 25 satellites" qualifier ("on an operating basis," excluding capex) was introduced in Q4 2024 Q&A and never foregrounded again. **Severity: High. Both companies.**

**3. Timeline slip pattern without systematic acknowledgment**
RKLB's Neutron has slipped H2 2024 → mid-2025 → H2 2025 → Q4 2026. Each slip explained by discrete factors; no forecasting methodology post-mortem. ASTS's six-satellites-per-month manufacturing target deferred from "H2 2025" to "Q4 2025" without explicit acknowledgment; actual Q1–Q2 2026 cadence suggests further deferral. Pattern is structural in both companies. **Severity: Medium-High. Both companies.**

**4. Equity dilution as primary funding mechanism**
RKLB: $303.8M ATM (Q2 2025) + $468.8M ATM (Q3 2025). Total equity grew from $382.5M to $1,721.8M in five quarters — a 350% increase driven by issuance, not earnings. ASTS: $1.15B convertible notes (Q2 2026); ongoing equity offerings. Both companies are funding operations through capital markets. Sustained negative FCF means both are permanently reliant on investor appetite. **Severity: High. Both companies.**

**5. Cash figure opacity**
RKLB: total equity and cash trajectory are straightforwardly disclosed relative to ASTS. ASTS: management-cited cash figures ($3.5B Q1 2026; $3.7B Q2 2026 pro forma) exceed structured balance sheet figures ($3,029.6M and $2,288.3M respectively) by $470M–$1.4B without explicit reconciliation. This gap requires disclosure of restricted cash, undrawn facilities, and timing of recently closed transactions. The blending of balance sheet cash with available-but-undrawn capacity is a disclosure hygiene failure. **Severity: High. ASTS-specific, but a sector signal about capital-intensive pre-revenue companies.**

### Risks Isolated to Single Companies

**RKLB-specific:**
- Constellation strategy ("Phase 3") is unfalsifiable after 8+ quarters. No application defined, no timeline, no capital budget, no anchor customer. Investors pricing the stock on this thesis have no checkpoint.
- Q4 2025 strategic theme proliferation (five new themes in one call) without milestones — matches prior aspirational theme pattern.
- Prepared-remarks/Q&A candor split is structural: promotional scripting in prepared remarks, genuine precision in Q&A. Investors should weight Q&A disclosures more heavily.
- Neutron budget overrun (30%+, ~$360M vs. $250–300M original) disclosed proactively — genuine positive flag on accountability — but timeline slippage has not received equivalent formal acknowledgment.

**ASTS-specific:**
- MNO partner metric (now "60 partners, 3 billion subscribers") has run 11 consecutive quarters as a credibility anchor with only 3 named commercial-structure counterparties. If this metric is marketing rather than commercial pipeline, it has been the primary non-financial credibility signal for the company's entire public history.
- FCF burn of -$719.2M in Q2 2026 (annualized >$2.8B) unacknowledged in prepared remarks. Non-disclosure of GAAP operating loss and FCF in prepared remarks at this burn rate is a material communication failure.
- Bluebird 7 satellite loss (Q1 2026) acknowledged briefly with no technical disclosure — mirrors the opposite of RKLB's anomaly-disclosure culture.
- Convertible note conversion price ($149.20) is "well above our all-time high trading price" per CFO — no scenario analysis of capital structure implications if conversion does not occur.
- Reflexive promotional intensification: CEO/President language has become more aspirational as commercial accountability moments approach, not less. This is the highest-risk communication pattern in the sector.

### The Gerstner Check

**What the numbers say about the narrative:**

The sector has generated $852.6M in revenue against $2,162.1M in capital expenditure and $2,546.3M in negative free cash flow over comparable recent periods. This means the sector has consumed approximately **$2.50–3.00 in capital for every $1.00 of revenue generated** in the structured data window.

ASTS drives 92% of sector CapEx ($1,984.1M of $2,162.1M) while generating 14% of sector revenue ($118.3M of $852.6M). RKLB generates 86% of sector revenue while deploying 8% of sector CapEx. These are fundamentally different capital models operating under the same sector label.

**RKLB capital model:** Revenue-generating with improving trajectory ($132M → $179.7M over five quarters, 36% growth); operating losses stable in absolute terms (-$51M to -$59M range) despite revenue scaling — which means losses are not shrinking as a percentage of revenue at the operating line (from -38.9% to -28.4%, so some improvement). CapEx is accelerating (-$21.6M → -$49.7M) as Neutron construction scales. The model is spending its way toward a second launch vehicle while the first funds operations.

**ASTS capital model:** Pre-commercial revenue ($1.9M → $54.3M → $31.5M, volatile and not yet ramping smoothly) against CapEx that has escalated from -$82M to -$622M per quarter. The $622.1M Q2 2026 CapEx figure — in a quarter that generated $31.5M in revenue — represents a 20:1 capital-to-revenue ratio. Operating losses are accelerating (-$58.7M → -$171.7M), not stabilizing. This is a pre-commercial infrastructure build that has not yet demonstrated revenue-generation capability at meaningful scale.

**Pundit claim calibration:** Sector-level space investment narratives that aggregate RKLB's operating-business characteristics with ASTS's infrastructure-build characteristics into a single "space economy" growth story are misleading. These are not the same type of company. RKLB has demonstrated a functioning, scaling launch and space systems business with real contracts. ASTS is a capital-intensive bet on a satellite architecture that has not yet demonstrated sustainable commercial revenue. Treating both as "space companies benefiting from tailwinds" obscures the $2.4B difference in their capital efficiency profiles.

**The operating loss stability puzzle (RKLB):** RKLB's operating income held within a -$51M to -$59.6M range for five consecutive quarters even as revenue grew 36%. This is a structural signal: either costs are scaling proportionally with revenue (the mix shift toward higher-cost Space Systems contracts absorbs the margin), or the company's cost structure is not leveraging as expected. Management has not directly addressed this in prepared remarks. It deserves explicit quarterly scrutiny.
