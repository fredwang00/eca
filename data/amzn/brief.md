## Candor Trajectory

**Grade progression:** Q2 2023: B (2.85) → Q3 2023: B (3.25) → Q4 2023: B (3.25) → Q1 2024: C (2.25) → Q2 2024: B+ (3.40) → Q3 2024: B+ (3.235) → Q4 2024: B (3.05) → Q1 2025: B (2.80) → Q2 2025: B (2.80) → Q3 2025: B+ (3.25) → Q4 2025: B+ (3.295) → Q1 2026: A− (3.455) → Q2 2026: A (3.65) → Annual Letter FY2025: A (3.65)

**Direction:** Improving, with meaningful acceleration in 2026.

**Named inflection points:**

- **Q1 2024 (floor, 2.25):** A heavily abridged transcript covering only six analyst questions produced the worst score in the series. Systematic Q&A deflection on CapEx intensity, AWS reacceleration, and grocery milestones. Strategic claims were unanchored and Q&A substituted length for precision.

- **Q2 2024 (first recovery, 3.40):** Strategic Clarity dimension upgraded to A on the strength of the explicit 90%-on-premises thesis, quantified Graviton benchmarks, and the AWS backlog disclosure ($156.6B). The recovery was real but FOG remained in prepared-remarks transition language.

- **Q4 2024 (brief regression, 3.05):** Despite strong financial results, the FOG Index dropped to C as Jassy's AI commentary became more promotional ("AI represents for sure the biggest opportunity since cloud"). The ROIC/CapEx discipline question went unanswered — the most consequential unresolved communication risk in the series.

- **Q1 2026 (structural improvement, 3.455):** Jassy finally addressed the ROIC/FCF framework question that had been evaded since Q4 2024, providing a concrete cash-cycle model (6–24 month monetization lag, 30-year data center lives). This resolved the primary communication-level risk carried across six quarters.

- **Q2 2026 (series high, 3.65):** Olsavsky proactively disclosed two non-recurring benefits (~$600M tariff refunds, ~$600M derivative gain) before any analyst asked, provided the "clean" AWS margin excluding the derivative, and raised CapEx guidance with attribution to a specific cause (memory cost inflation). Financial Candor and Strategic Clarity both graded A.

## FOG Patterns

**Recurring phrases across the series:**

1. **"It's still early days"** — used as a universal hedge for Alexa+, Gen AI, grocery, healthcare, and agentic commerce. Appears in every quarter from Q2 2023 through Q2 2026 without modification or graduation to more specific language.

2. **"Very strong start" / "off to a really good start"** — applied to Amazon Haul (Q4 2024), Prime Video ads (Q1 2024), Alexa+ (Q1 2025), and Amazon Now (Q2 2026). No metric follows this phrase in any quarter.

3. **"Extraordinarily / very unusual opportunity"** — appears in Q3 2024 ("once in a lifetime type of opportunity"), Q4 2025 ("extraordinarily unusual opportunity"), and Q1 2026 ("very unusual"). Functions as a conversation-stopper when capital discipline questions escalate.

4. **"Every single one of our businesses has [AI initiative]"** — Q2 2023 version: "Every single one of our businesses inside of Amazon has multiple generative AI initiatives." Q1 2026 version: "I do not see a place in any of our businesses...where we are not going to have giant impact." Iterates across quarters with no discriminating content added.

5. **"Our DNA starts with the customer"** and variants — Q2 2023, Q3 2024, Q2 2025. Amazon's 25-year self-narrative, repeated as a competitive differentiator claim without new evidence.

6. **"We're not even at the [baseball metaphor] of [transformational technology]"** — Q1 2025: "not even at the second strike of the first batter in the first inning." Deploys in lieu of market sizing or adoption curves.

**Linguistic precision trajectory:** Prepared remarks improved materially from Q2 2023 to Q2 2026, particularly in Olsavsky's CFO sections, which are largely FOG-free throughout. Jassy's Q&A responses show the sharpest improvement — the Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 calls feature substantive capital-cycle pedagogy replacing Q4 2024's promotional register. FOG remains concentrated in two persistent zones: (1) transitional language between business segments in prepared remarks, and (2) responses to competitive positioning questions about AWS market share.

**Notable regression:** Q4 2024 saw the FOG Index drop to C — the only call where financial strength and communication vagueness diverged sharply in opposite directions.

## Flags & Risks

**Structural data flags:**
- FCF turned negative in Q1 2025 (−$8,004M) and near-zero in Q2 2025 ($332M) and Q3 2025 ($430M). Recovered to $14,937M in Q4 2025 but turned negative again in Q1 2026 (−$18,171M). This is not a distress signal — the company's operating cash flow and equity are both rising — but the declared strategy of "FCF compression ahead of monetization" is now measurable in the data and should be tracked against the 2027-2028 recovery thesis.
- Cash declined from $78,779M (Q4 2024) to $57,741M (Q2 2025) before recovering to $101,816M (Q1 2026). The Q2 2025 trough coincides with peak quarterly CapEx acceleration.
- No equity-decline flag: equity rose $156B over the evaluation window.

**Communication-level risks (aggregated):**

- **ROIC question resolution is untested under pressure.** The Q1 2026 call's apparent improvement on capital discipline language occurred with only six analyst questions and no adversarial follow-up. The Q4 2025 ROIC question was deflected; Q1 2026 answered it with structural logic but was not re-tested. If Q2 2026 or Q3 2026 features a return engagement with the same question, and the structural answer holds, the resolution is genuine. If it reverts to conviction language, Q4 2025's deflection was not resolved.

- **The "$50B hypothetical chips" figure is an unanchored new metric** introduced in Q1 2026 without accounting reconciliation. The actual $20B ARR is real; the $50B standalone construct depends on unstated transfer-pricing assumptions and a business model Amazon does not operate. This fits the "perishable goal" pattern.

- **FTC settlement ($2.5B, Q3 2025) disclosed as a line item but never explained.** No description of what conduct it covers, what behavioral changes it requires, or how management thinks about compliance culture. A governance event treated as an accounting entry.

- **Alexa+ has generated exclusively qualitative positive language** across four consecutive quarters without a single retention metric, task-completion rate, subscription conversion figure, or revenue target. Classic perishable goal risk.

- **AWS operating margin is no longer explicitly stated as a percentage** in Q1 2026, requiring back-calculation. This is a mild regression from Q4 2025, which provided explicit margin commentary with forward guidance that margins "will fluctuate."

- **Tariff exposure is conspicuously absent from Q1 2026 discussion** despite being listed in the safe harbor disclaimer. No analyst asked; management did not volunteer. For a company with material imported-goods exposure, this is a gap.

- **The "substantial portion" of $200B (now $220B) CapEx with customer commitments** — the precise percentage is never disclosed despite the scale (Annual Letter language; carried into Q2 2026). 60% vs. 90% committed implies materially different risk profiles.

## Verify Next Quarter

From Q2 2026 and Q1 2026 tracking commitments, verify in the Q3 2026 call:

1. **CapEx trajectory:** Does the $220B 2026 CapEx figure hold, or does memory cost inflation or demand signals push it higher? Is FCF guidance introduced now that the data center monetization timeline is clarifying?

2. **"Middle of the barbell" demand materialization:** Does Jassy's language shift from "enterprises evaluating" to "enterprises in production" at scale? Any metric distinguishing frontier lab demand from traditional enterprise production workloads would validate or challenge the core long-term growth thesis.

3. **AWS operating margin disclosure:** Is the margin stated explicitly as a percentage, or has back-calculation from raw figures become the new normal? If another derivative accounting gain or unusual item appears, does Olsavsky provide the same proactive clean/reported disaggregation he demonstrated in Q2 2026?

4. **Kiro and Amazon Q traction:** Kiro "tripled in usage quarter-over-quarter" (Q2 2026); Amazon Q has 15+ enterprise customers in production. Do these metrics graduate to revenue attribution or retention data, or do they disappear — the primary perishable metric test for both products.

5. **Trainium third-party sales:** Jassy said "I expect there's a real chance we'll do that in the future" regarding selling Trainium directly to third-party data centers. If not revisited, flag as introduced-and-dropped.

6. **LEO commercial launch:** Jassy stated "commercial service on track to launch in a few months" (Q1 2026, implying Q3). Q3 call should confirm whether commercial launch occurred and whether Q4 capitalization transition is on schedule.

7. **The adversarial ROIC test:** If analysts re-engage on capital discipline and management provides the same structural cash-cycle framework from Q1 2026, the resolution is genuine. Reversion to "extraordinarily unusual opportunity" language confirms Q4 2025's deflection pattern was not resolved.

8. **The $50B hypothetical chips figure:** Does it reappear with accounting reconciliation, or does it vanish — the definitional test for perishable goal status.
