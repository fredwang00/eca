## Candor Trajectory

**Grade progression:** Q2 2023: B (2.85) → Q3 2023: B (3.25) → Q4 2023: B (3.25) → Q1 2024: C (2.25) → Q2 2024: B+ (3.40) → Q3 2024: B+ (3.235) → Q4 2024: B (3.05) → Q1 2025: B (2.80) → Q2 2025: B (2.80) → Q3 2025: B+ (3.25) → Q4 2025: B+ (3.295) → Q1 2026: A− (3.455) → Q2 2026: A (3.65) → Annual Letter FY2025: A (3.65)

**Direction: Improving**, with one clear trough and a sustained late-period ascent.

**Inflection points:**
- *Q1 2024 (trough):* The only C composite in the series. An apparently abridged transcript, systematic Q&A deflection on every quantitative question (CapEx intensity, AI revenue, North America margin trajectory), and zero falsifiable forward claims. AWS reacceleration narrative was asserted without evidence.
- *Q2 2024 (recovery):* Return to B+ driven by explicit AWS backlog disclosure ($156.6B), Jassy's direct answers on AWS capacity economics, and the first falsifiable pharmacy milestone ("more than a dozen cities by year-end"). The inputs-vs-outputs framework solidified as a communication architecture.
- *Q1 2026 (breakout):* The ROIC/FCF question that went unanswered in Q4 2025 received substantive structural treatment — six-to-24-month monetization lag, 30-year data center lives, explicit FCF headwind acknowledgment. AWS backlog disclosed proactively at $364B. First composite above 3.40 in the earnings call series.
- *Q2 2026 (peak):* Olsavsky proactively flagged two non-recurring items ($600M tariff refund, $600M derivative gain) before analyst questions, provided clean AWS margin ex-derivative, and raised CapEx guidance with a specific cause (memory cost inflation). Matches the Annual Letter grade.

The two-voice pattern — Olsavsky providing financial precision, Jassy providing strategic promotion — is the series' defining structural dynamic. The trajectory improves most when Jassy's Q&A answers adopt Olsavsky's specificity register; it degrades when Jassy substitutes fluency for falsifiability.

## FOG Patterns

**Recurring phrases across quarters (linguistic fingerprint):**

1. **"It's still early days"** — used in Q2 2023 (gen AI), Q1 2024 (Prime Video ads), Q1 2025 (Alexa+), Q2 2025 (AWS competitive response). Functions as a universal accountability hedge; appears whenever a quantitative milestone is requested and not provided.

2. **"Every single one of our businesses"** — Q2 2023 ("every single one has multiple generative AI initiatives"), Q4 2024 ("every single one of these functions...are going to very significantly change"). Rhetorical intensifier that sweeps past specificity.

3. **"Strong start" / "off to a really good start" / "very strong start"** — Amazon Haul (Q4 2024), Prime Video ads (Q1 2024), Alexa+ (Q1 2025). Applied consistently to new product launches without a benchmark, metric, or baseline. What would a weak start look like?

4. **"Very unusual opportunity" / "extraordinarily unusual opportunity" / "once in a lifetime"** — Q3 2024 (gen AI CapEx), Q4 2025 (capital discipline Q&A, used twice in proximity), Q2 2026. Peaks precisely when capital discipline questions intensify; functions as a conversation-stopper rather than an argument.

5. **"Price, selection, and convenience"** — present in Q2 2023 and throughout. Amazon's three-word mantra repeated so frequently it has become a verbal tic rather than strategic communication.

6. **"Multi-billion dollar revenue run rate" / "triple-digit year-over-year growth"** — used for AI/AWS in Q1 2025, Q2 2025, and Q3 2024 without ever naming the actual number. A compound hedge: large enough to sound impressive, vague enough to be unverifiable.

**Trend:** Linguistic precision improved meaningfully in the prepared remarks track from Q3 2024 onward, particularly in Olsavsky's financial sections. Q&A FOG remained elevated through Q2 2025 and only partially resolved in Q1–Q2 2026, where the capital-discipline deflection pattern was replaced with structural framing — but universal vision claims ("I passionately believe every customer experience will be reinvented with AI") continue to appear in closing remarks regardless of overall call quality. The FOG is now concentrated in transitional and closing language rather than in substantive disclosure zones, which represents genuine improvement.

## Flags & Risks

**Data quality flags:**
- FCF is deeply negative in Q1 2026 (−$18.2B structured) and near zero in Q2–Q3 2025 ($332M, $430M), contrasting sharply with strong operating income. Management acknowledges the FCF/CapEx timing mismatch explicitly from Q1 2026 onward, but no FCF floor or threshold has been committed to.
- The $50B hypothetical "standalone chips business" figure introduced in Q1 2026 is not reconciled to any reported financial metric; it is a transfer-pricing construct, not an accounting line. If it disappears in Q2 2026 tracking without explanation, that confirms perishable goal status.
- Q1 2024 analysis flagged as a "significantly abridged transcript" — scores on dimensions 2–5 may understate the underlying call quality, but the deflection pattern on quantitative questions is documented across available content.
- Q1 2025 includes non-recurring one-time charges quantified by Olsavsky; management disclosed them proactively and presented adjusted margins (credit), but offered no causal explanation for "historical customer returns that have not yet been resolved."
- Q3 2025 includes a $2.5B FTC settlement and $1.8B severance disclosed as line items. Neither received substantive behavioral or governance treatment in the call.

**Communication-level risks (synthesized):**
- **AI revenue opacity is a structural pattern.** "Multi-billion dollar, triple-digit growth" has been the AI revenue formulation across Q3 2024, Q4 2024, Q1 2025, and Q2 2025. The $20B+ chips ARR (Q1 2026) and $25B+ (Q2 2026) represent genuine progress but arrived 18 months after analysts began asking. The deliberate avoidance of a discrete AI revenue line is unlikely to be incidental at this point.
- **Alexa+ is a perishable goal in formation.** Launched Q1 2025 with "100,000+ users" and enthusiastic but metric-free framing; described as having "millions of customers" by Q2 2025 without retention data, task completion rates, or revenue contribution. No commercial model progress against the $9.99/non-Prime price point has been disclosed. Now three quarters into the rollout with no accountability anchors.
- **FTC settlement received no governance treatment.** A $2.5B regulatory settlement was disclosed as a financial line item in Q3 2025 and Q4 2025 without any description of what conduct was at issue, what behavioral changes are required, or how leadership frames the compliance culture that produced it.
- **The ROIC question is partially but not fully resolved.** Q1 2026 provided the structural framework (6–24 month cash cycle, 30-year data center lives); Q2 2026 added server break-even math (~3 years). No minimum FCF threshold, ROIC target, or CapEx ceiling has been stated. Management continues to raise CapEx guidance ($105B → $200B → $220B) without a quantitative constraint.
- **AWS competitive claims remain evidence-free.** "Strongest security," "most functionality by a large amount," "highest reported code acceptance rates" — these have appeared across Q3 2023, Q4 2024, Q2 2025, and Q3 2025. None cite independent benchmarks. The "adventures" euphemism for competitor security incidents in Q2 2025 is the lowest point in the series for competitive candor.

## Verify Next Quarter

Based on Q2 2026 tracking commitments and open items from the full series:

1. **CapEx and FCF trajectory.** Jassy raised full-year 2026 CapEx to ~$220B and explicitly stated capacity will be constrained through 2027. Verify: Is Q3 CapEx tracking to the ~$55B/quarter implied run rate, or moving higher? Does management introduce any FCF guidance or threshold alongside operating income guidance? The absence of FCF guidance when FCF is the central investor concern is itself a signal.

2. **"Middle of the barbell" demand evidence.** Jassy's bull case depends on enterprise production AI workloads materializing at scale. The Q3 call should be checked for whether AWS commentary shifts language from "enterprises evaluating" to "enterprises in production" as the dominant characterization — or whether the barbell middle remains aspirational.

3. **AWS margin sustainability ex-one-time items.** Q2 2026 AWS margin was ~39% including ~$600M derivative gain (520 bps ex-derivative). As capacity scales and depreciation loads increase, monitor whether Olsavsky provides the same disaggregation if another derivative gain appears, and whether the underlying margin trajectory is explicitly addressed.

4. **LEO/Kuiper commercial launch.** Jassy said commercial launch was "on track in a few months" (i.e., Q3 2026). Olsavsky said capitalization of manufacturing and launch costs begins in Q4. Verify: (a) Did commercial service launch? (b) What is the explicit Q3 LEO cost figure? (c) Does the Q4 capitalization transition appear in segment financials as promised?

5. **The $50B hypothetical chips figure.** Introduced in Q1 2026 without accounting reconciliation. If it does not reappear in Q2 2026 with a defined methodology, or disappears entirely, that confirms perishable goal status. The $25B+ actual ARR figure is the one to track for sequential growth.

6. **Trainium third-party sales.** Jassy said "there's a real chance we'll do that in the future" (selling Trainium to external data centers). If this is not revisited in Q3 — either progressed or explicitly deferred — it qualifies as an introduced-and-dropped commitment.

7. **Adversarial Q&A test on capital discipline.** If analysts return to CapEx governors or FCF floors and management provides the structural framework from Q1 2026 rather than reverting to conviction language ("extraordinarily unusual opportunity"), that confirms the candor improvement is genuine rather than an artifact of an easier Q&A session.
